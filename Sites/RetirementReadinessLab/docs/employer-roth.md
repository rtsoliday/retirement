# Employer Roth 401(k)/403(b) support

Add each designated Roth plan under **Account balances → Employer Roth accounts**. Its balance is additional to the entered Roth IRA and pre-tax balances. Each record retains its owner, account type, remaining after-tax principal (including conversions), first funding tax year, future deposits and conversion history. Unknown balances and history remain draft inputs and block a forecast until resolved. Copies, JSON backups, detailed assumptions, result fingerprints and reports include these records.

## Calculation behavior

- Investment returns apply to each plan; deposits increase both balance and basis. Employee and Roth-designated employer deposits stop at the owner's retirement or death, or when a modeled full rollover closes the plan. Deposits are funded outside the simulation, with no employment-tax or contribution-limit calculation.
- Ordinary spending uses eligible pre-tax savings, Roth IRAs, available employer Roth plans in input order, taxable savings, then cash. The existing drawdown option can put cash first. The declared access date gates employer withdrawals; blank means the owner's forecast retirement. An inaccessible balance can remain invested when a path runs short.
- Employer distributions recover basis and earnings proportionally. Nonqualified earnings enter federal income tax, taxable Social Security and Medicare estimates/lookback. Qualification uses that plan's five-calendar-tax-year period plus age 59½, death or declared disability. No owner RMDs are taken from designated Roth accounts.
- Before 59½, enabled penalties apply to nonqualified earnings and taxable in-plan conversion principal within its separate five-year period. Recovered basis consumes non-conversion principal first unless the plan pays from a separately maintained conversion source; conversion principal then follows oldest-year, taxable-first ordering. Plan-specific Rule of 55 and owner disability declarations can remove the penalty, while earnings can remain taxable. Declared disability also follows that owner’s IRA/pre-tax distributions and IRA qualification after rollover; Social Security disability benefits are not modeled. Rule of 55 requires this employer's actual/planned separation in or after the year the owner turns 55. It does not apply to the receiving Roth IRA.
- A full direct rollover moves the remaining plan balance to the owner's Roth IRA without increasing total savings or taxable income. Qualified value becomes IRA principal; nonqualified full transfers carry remaining after-tax basis. Recent in-plan conversion history survives for recapture. The receiving IRA retains its existing funding year, or starts in the rollover year if never funded. An employer clock does not establish the IRA clock. Transactions occur at the first modeled monthly boundary on/after the entered date.
- A planned in-plan conversion moves up to the requested amount from the owner's available pre-tax pool into that plan, after reserving unmet RMDs. It creates ordinary income and a new conversion lot. Its tax and any penalties incurred funding it are withdrawn separately after that month's spending, in cash → taxable → Roth IRA → available employer Roth → pre-tax order. SEPP-protected pre-tax accounts cannot fund it. This is limited to the retirement forecast; the working-life tax return is not modeled.

## Compatibility and limits

Old plans normalize missing employer records to an empty array and retain their previous engine and results. No saved Roth IRA amount is automatically moved to an employer account. When an employer record is added to a legacy pooled plan, an internal owner ledger preserves the old pre-tax/IRA balances as primary-owned and uses the shared retirement date; employer plans retain their entered owners. Inactive separate-person draft inputs are not reinterpreted. Converting the plan to separate-person inputs remains an explicit choice.

In plans with employer records, IRA and employer qualification/recapture clocks use actual calendar tax years. Annual federal income-tax, RMD, filing-status and Medicare history periods retain forecast anniversaries. This does not turn the simulator into a calendar tax-return calculator. Roth principal can exceed its balance after losses; a full direct rollover preserves unrecovered basis even when it exceeds the transferred value. The model uses the same household investment allocation for these plans.

The user declares provider-permitted access, direct-rollover eligibility and conversion eligibility. A planned in-plan conversion assumes that the entered pre-tax source belongs to the same employer. The app cannot verify that source against the aggregate pre-tax pool. Surviving-spouse inheritance becomes a simplified own-plan assumption after the death year, retaining participation and conversion history and clearing the original employer's early-access declaration. Actual beneficiary elections are not reconstructed.

Plan loans, hardship distributions, partial rollovers, plan-to-plan transfers, non-spouse beneficiary elections, contribution-limit checks, non-Roth after-tax basis, employment taxes and state/local income taxes remain excluded. Money already rolled over before today belongs in the receiving IRA/history, rather than as a future transaction. A full modeled rollover closes the record and stops future deposits; enter another plan record for later savings into a different employer plan.

Rules are grounded in the [IRS designated Roth FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-faqs-on-designated-roth-accounts), [Notice 2010-84 Q12–13](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2010-51_IRB), [rollover regulations](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-22_IRB), [early-distribution exceptions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions), and [current RMD FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plan-and-ira-required-minimum-distributions-faqs).

Existing no-employer calculation regressions still pass.
